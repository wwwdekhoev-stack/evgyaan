Bailiffs have searched two BYD offices in the Netherlands because Jaguar Land Rover (JLR) believes the BYD Ti7 copies the Defender. The raids happened in August and became public only this week, just before a court hearing in The Hague.

## What actually happened

Court-appointed bailiffs working for JLR took copies of digital files at BYD's two offices, in Schiedam and Hoofddorp. Dutch law lets a judge allow evidence to be secured before the main case starts, and JLR argued the records could otherwise be destroyed.

JLR suspects BYD took a Defender apart to learn how some of its technology works, then used that in the Ti7. It has not said which patents or designs it means, and no court document is public. BYD's lawyers reportedly describe the search as a "fishing expedition" and argue that far too much was taken.

This week's hearing is about procedure, mainly which of the seized records JLR may use. It cannot answer the bigger question of whether any copying took place.

## Why the Ti7 gets compared with the Defender

The Ti7 has a squared-off body, an upright stance and a spare wheel on the tailgate, which is why everyone reaches for the Defender comparison.

In China the Ti7 launched on 9 September 2025 under the name Fangchengbao Tai 7, priced from about $26,900. In the UK it costs £47,995 for a single all-wheel-drive trim with 7 seats, 408PS and 0 to 100 km/h in 4.8 seconds. It is a plug-in hybrid, not a pure EV: a 35.6 kWh Blade battery gives about 119 km on electricity, and a 1.5-litre petrol engine takes over after that. UK deliveries start in January 2027.

Here is the price gap that makes JLR nervous:

- BYD Ti7, 7 seats: £47,995
- Land Rover Defender 110, 7 seats: from £68,465
- Difference: £20,470

## Why JLR is serious about this

The Defender is a big earner for JLR. Between July and September 2026 it sold 29,500 Defenders, against 25,370 in the same months of 2025. That is more than a third of the company's 79,000 retail sales in the quarter.

JLR has done this before. It took on the Landwind X7 in China, and in 2019 a Beijing court ruled that the car borrowed five design features from the Range Rover Evoque.

Looks are hard to protect. A 2020 ruling in England's High Court backed the refusal to register the old Defender's shape as a trade mark.

## The EVGyan take

Boxy SUVs are not new, so a lookalike body alone is a weak case. The claim that BYD studied a Defender's technology is the harder fight, and the files taken from BYD's offices may decide it.

For Indian buyers, nothing changes today, because no India launch has been announced for the Ti7. It does show how quickly Chinese brands now build rivals to famous SUVs at prices £20,000 lower.
