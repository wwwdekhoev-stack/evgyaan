VinFast registered 2,571 electric cars in India in October 2026, up 1,710.56% from the 142 units it managed in October 2025. That makes the Vietnamese brand India's fourth-biggest EV brand by registrations for the month, ahead of Kia, Maruti, Hyundai and BYD, roughly 15 months after it opened its first showroom here.

The VinFast India registrations figure comes from FADA's October retail data, carried by Rushlane and Autopunditz among others, and all of them print the same numbers.

## Fourth place, and BYD is nowhere near

Here is how the October table looked:

- Tata: 14,669 (+77.61%)
- Mahindra: 8,538 (+85.65%)
- MG: 5,939 (+10.80%)
- VinFast: 2,571 (+1,710.56%)
- Kia: 1,395 (+85.26%)
- Maruti: 1,200
- Hyundai: 760 (+42.32%)
- BYD: 737 (+4.10%)

That is about 3.5 times BYD's count, from a brand that has been here far longer and advertises much harder. Put another way, VinFast took 6.9% of every electric car registered in the country in October. A year ago that share was 0.7%.

Total electric car registrations hit 37,049 in October, up 76.47%, and EVs were 7.3% of all passenger car registrations against 3.5% a year earlier. But the overall car market actually shrank 14.23% that month, so EVs were growing while everything else was not.

## The part the growth number hides

A 1,710% jump looks enormous mainly because the base was tiny. October was also not VinFast's best month: registrations fell 13.26% from the 2,964 it did in September.

Service reach is the bigger question. VinFast had 24 operational dealerships in November 2025 by its own announcement. A November 2026 listing shows around 70 showrooms and 85 service centres. That is quick work, but Tata and Mahindra count their touchpoints in the hundreds, and a brand with no resale history yet is still asking buyers to take something on trust.

The factory suggests VinFast is not treating India as a trial. Its Thoothukudi plant in Tamil Nadu started with capacity for 50,000 vehicles a year, scalable to 150,000, against a committed ₹16,000 crore in phases with ₹4,300 crore in the first, as reported by Business Standard. At the opening, VinFast's chief executive said he wanted the site to become the company's biggest export base serving Africa, the Middle East and the countries around India.

## What this means for you

The VF 6 starts at ₹18.19 lakh and runs to ₹20.09 lakh ex-showroom, with a 59.6 kWh pack, a claimed 468 km and Level-2 ADAS. The Tata Curvv EV sits at ₹17.19 lakh to ₹19.69 lakh with 55 kWh and an ARAI-claimed 585 km. The MG Windsor EV starts lower still, at ₹14.85 lakh.

So VinFast is asking for a bit more than Tata and quite a bit more than MG, and claiming less range than the Curvv. What you get back is more power and a longer features list. Whether that is worth it depends on how close your nearest VinFast service centre is, and that is worth checking before you book.
