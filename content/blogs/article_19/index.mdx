Hyundai's small electric SUV for India finally has a name, or at least a very loud hint of one. On its third-quarter earnings call on 29 October, Hyundai Motor's management listed its next India launches as the Bayon and the "IONIQ P". That's the first time the Hyundai Ioniq P name has come up for the made-in-India EV that has been testing on our roads for months.

One small catch. Hyundai Motor India hasn't officially confirmed the name yet. But when the parent company says it to investors, it's usually more than a slip of the tongue.

## What Hyundai has actually confirmed

Here's what the company itself has put on record:

- Codename HE1i, a compact electric SUV designed and localised for India
- Debut by March 2027, which is the last quarter of Hyundai's FY27. Autocar India's sources point to January.
- Level 2 ADAS and a new-generation infotainment system
- It sits below the Creta Electric, and becomes Hyundai's third EV in India after the Ioniq 5 and the Creta Electric

The timeline has moved around a bit. Hyundai first said March 2027, then Hyundai India CEO Tarun Garg talked about the end of 2026, and now early 2027 looks like the target. Either way, it's close.

## Battery and range: still a guessing game

Hyundai hasn't shared battery or range figures, so treat any number you see online with a pinch of salt.

[Autocar India reports](https://www.autocarindia.com/car-news/hyundai-compact-ev-to-debut-in-q1-2027-441068) that the car uses the E-GMP (K) platform from the Hyundai Inster sold abroad, and will come in Standard Range and Long Range versions. It also reports that early cars will use Svolt battery cells, with a shift to locally made cells planned later. [CarWale expects](https://www.carwale.com/news/hyundai-ioniq-p-to-be-the-name-of-the-small-ev-for-india/) the Ioniq P to share a lot with its Kia cousin, the Syros EV, which offers 42 kWh and 51.4 kWh packs and a claimed range of up to 526 km. We'll know which guess is right when Hyundai pulls the cover off.

## Who it has to beat

The Ioniq P walks into one of the busiest EV segments in India. The Tata Nexon EV, Mahindra XUV 3XO EV and Kia Syros EV are already here, and the Syros EV starts at Rs 13.49 lakh (ex-showroom). That's roughly the price bar Hyundai has to match or beat, and heavy local production is how it plans to get there.

## The EVGyan take

Hyundai has been late to mass-market EVs in India. According to Autocar India, EVs made up only about 1 percent of Hyundai's India sales in FY26. The Ioniq 5 is a premium product, and the Creta Electric plays a segment higher. A locally built, Nexon-sized EV is the car that can actually move that number, and Hyundai has 26 launches lined up for India by 2030 to back it up.

For buyers, more competition in the Rs 13–20 lakh EV space only means sharper prices and better features. If you're shopping in this budget and aren't in a rush, waiting for the Ioniq P's price in early 2027 won't hurt.
