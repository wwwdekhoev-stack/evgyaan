The Ultraviolette Tesseract finally stood in front of a crowd this week. The catch: the crowd was in Milan, not Mumbai. The scooter that collected 20,000 pre-bookings inside 48 hours of its March 2025 unveil got its world premiere at EICMA 2026 on 3 November, in a Europe-spec form that Italian and Hungarian coverage calls the Tesseract Maxi.

Same 6 kWh pack. Same 15 kW motor. New 100-volt electrical system, shared with the X-47 Venom motorcycle Ultraviolette launched in India on 23 October at an introductory ₹2.65 lakh.

## What Milan actually saw

Specs for the Europe-spec Tesseract Maxi, as reported by Vaielettrico and Sportmotor, both on 3 November:

- 6 kWh battery on the new 100-volt architecture
- 15 kW peak motor, about 20 hp
- 32 litres of underseat storage
- Charges to 80% in under an hour
- 7-inch touchscreen with navigation
- Hypersense radar for blind-spot warning, lane-change and overtaking assist
- Built-in dashcam, traction control, 14-inch wheels

Range and top speed for the Europe version were not announced.

## The India version, and the wait

Ultraviolette's own Tesseract page still lists three packs: 3.5 kWh, 5 kWh and 6 kWh, with IDC range of 162 km, 220 km and 261 km. Top speed up to 125 kph. Thirty-four litres under the seat, two more than the Milan bike. 20-80% in under 30 minutes on a Supernova charger. Battery warranty of eight years or 2 lakh km.

Then there is the line that matters. That same page says the variants and the full spec sheet only get locked down in Q1 2027, and deliveries follow after that.

At the March 2025 unveil, deliveries were expected by early 2026. The price was ₹1.45 lakh, with the first 50,000 units offered at ₹1.20 lakh. Autocar India's listing, updated 30 October, now shows a March 2027 launch and expected on-road Delhi prices of ₹1.57 lakh, ₹1.89 lakh and ₹2.16 lakh across the three packs.

## What this means for you

If you booked a Tesseract on day two, you have been waiting about 20 months, and you are looking at another three to five.

The delay did buy something. Ultraviolette has said the reworked scooter sits lower, carries a wider seat and gets a rebuilt floorboard, on top of the 100-volt system. A Tesseract that ships in 2027 with radar and a dashcam is a better scooter than the one that was due in early 2026 without them.

What the delay cost is shelf space. TVS registered 60,433 electric two-wheelers in October. Ather registered 31,795. Both have spent the waiting period putting scooters in showrooms.

## The EVGyan take

Showing it in Milan first is not a snub. Europe will pay European money for a scooter with radar and lane-change assist, and Ultraviolette already has the X-47 Venom on sale there to prove the point. India gets the volume version once the line is ready for it. Just don't plan your 2027 summer around it.
