Up to 75% of the next generation of Volkswagen, Skoda and Cupra cars could sit on an architecture VW built with Xpeng for China. That is the claim in a 27 October report by Germany's Manager Magazin, picked up by Reuters and CnEVPost. VW hasn't confirmed it, and the same report says there are doubts inside the company about the idea.

## Why the 75% number matters

VW and Xpeng's joint platform was sold as a China-only fix: a way for VW to catch up on software and launch speed in the world's toughest EV market. Using it in Europe would be an admission that the China-developed hardware is simply better value than what Wolfsburg built on its own.

Treat the 75% as a reported figure, not a plan. Platform decisions at VW have a habit of changing, and the internal pushback is already on record.

## Xpeng's Mona L05 goes global before it goes on sale in China

On 26 October, Xpeng said ([via CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/10/26/xpeng-starts-global-shipments-mona-l05/)) that global shipments of the Mona L05 had started, with a target of 65 countries and regions, three days before the car's own China launch on 29 October. The L05 is a 5-seat mid-size SUV, the third model in the budget Mona line and a step above the Mona L03 sedan. China pre-sale prices run from 169,800 to 191,800 yuan (roughly ₹21-24 lakh), across battery-electric and range-extender versions.

Shipping a car abroad before it launches at home is a new move even for Chinese brands. It says Xpeng wants Mona to be a volume play outside China from day one, not a premium-first story.

## Why this matters for India

Neither story touches India directly, yet. But the pattern is the same one we keep seeing: Chinese-developed platforms are becoming the base for non-Chinese brands, not just Chinese ones. Which platforms eventually land in India, through joint ventures or licensing, will be decided by deals like the VW-Xpeng one.
