Two separate Xpeng stories from the same week show just how central the Chinese EV maker has become to Volkswagen's global electric strategy — and to its own ambitions outside China.

## VW weighs bringing the Xpeng-co-developed platform to Europe

According to a report on 27 October, **Volkswagen is considering bringing to Europe the EV architecture it jointly developed with Xpeng** for the Chinese market. If this goes ahead, it could end up underpinning as much as **75% of the next generation of models** across VW, Skoda and Cupra.

That's a striking number. VW and Xpeng's China-focused joint development deal was originally framed as a China-specific fix — a way for VW to catch up on software and EV architecture speed in the world's toughest EV market. Bringing that same architecture home to Europe would be an admission that the China-developed platform is good enough, and fast enough to market, to use globally rather than only locally.

## Xpeng's Mona L05 starts global shipments

Separately, on 26 October, Xpeng confirmed that global shipments of the **Mona L05** (the international badge for the China-market Mona M03) have begun, with a target of reaching **65 countries and regions**.

The Mona sub-brand has been one of Xpeng's fastest-growing lines in China — a value-focused EV aimed at buyers who want Xpeng's smart-driving tech without the price tag of its flagship models. Taking it global this early suggests Xpeng wants to replicate that volume play outside China too, rather than only exporting its premium models.

## Why this matters for India

Neither VW's platform plan nor the Mona L05 rollout directly touches India yet. But both stories are part of the same pattern we keep seeing: Chinese EV technology and Chinese-developed platforms are increasingly becoming the backbone for non-Chinese global brands, not just Chinese ones. That's worth watching, because it shapes which platforms eventually do make their way into markets like India — directly or through joint ventures.
