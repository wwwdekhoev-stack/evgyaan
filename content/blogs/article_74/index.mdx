Ultraviolette is now selling an electric motorcycle in Europe, and the one it picked claims 343 km on a full charge.

The Bengaluru company took the X-47 Venom to EICMA in Milan on 3 November and opened it to European buyers. Autocar Professional reports this makes Ultraviolette the first Indian manufacturer retailing electric motorcycles in Europe. The Ultraviolette X-47 Venom Europe launch came alongside the Tesseract maxi-scooter, which was shown but not put on sale. Narayan Subramaniam, CEO and co-founder, said the company is "expanding our global portfolio".

## The numbers Ultraviolette is quoting

From the company's own spec page:

- Battery: 7.5 kWh on the X-47 and X-47+, 11 kWh on the Recon and Recon+
- Range (IDC): 227 km on the 7.5 kWh pack, 343 km on the 11 kWh pack
- Peak power: 30 kW, or 40 hp
- Wheel torque: 550 Nm
- Top speed: 155 kph
- 0-100 kph: 5.9 seconds
- Battery warranty: 8 years or 8,00,000 km

Every variant uses the new VX-100 architecture, which runs at 100 volts, with the SRB-11 battery. The Recon versions add Ultraviolette's Hypersense radar. There are dashcams front and back, a 7-inch touchscreen and cruise control. Kerb weight is 180 kg. No European price has been announced yet.

## Why a 3,631-unit home market pushed it abroad

Here is the part nobody on the Milan stand will say out loud. India registered 3,631 electric motorcycles in October 2026 against 2,23,886 electric two-wheelers in total. That is 1.6% of the segment (3,631 divided by 2,23,886 = 1.62%). Scooters do the volume in this country. Motorcycles do not, not yet.

So a company that builds only electric motorcycles has two options: wait for Indian riders to switch, or go find riders who already own big bikes. Ultraviolette has picked the second. It happened in the same week that TVS showed three Europe-bound electric scooters at the same show and said its Italian sales are up 110%.

## What this means for you

In India the X-47 Venom went on sale on 22 October at an introductory ₹2.65 lakh ex-showroom for the first 5,000 buyers, with deliveries starting this month. That sits under Royal Enfield's Flying Flea C6 at ₹2.79 lakh, and the radar and the bigger battery option come with it.

The useful signal for an Indian buyer is not the stand in Milan. It is that a bike built in Bengaluru now has to survive European warranty claims and European riders. Parts supply, software updates and service standards usually get better once that starts happening.
