The MG Hector Tomahawk EV costs ₹75,000 more. All four variants have moved up by exactly that much, and nothing has been added to the car in return. JSW MG Motor has let the introductory pricing lapse, roughly ten weeks after the seven-seater went on sale in August.

The entry Excite now asks ₹20.24 lakh ex-showroom instead of ₹19.49 lakh.

## The revised price list

- **Excite, 7-seater** — ₹20.24 lakh, was ₹19.49 lakh
- **Exclusive, 7-seater** — ₹22.74 lakh, was ₹21.99 lakh
- **Essence, 5-seater** — ₹23.74 lakh, was ₹22.99 lakh
- **Essence, 7-seater** — ₹24.24 lakh, was ₹23.49 lakh

All figures ex-showroom. The August launch pricing was flagged as a limited-period offer at the time, so this one was coming.

## The battery rental plan did not move

Here is the part worth your attention. MG's Battery-as-a-Service pricing has stayed exactly where it was. The Excite still starts at ₹13.99 lakh upfront, and you still pay ₹4.9 per km for the battery you do not own.

Only the outright price went up, so the gap between the two routes has widened. On the Excite it used to be ₹5.50 lakh. It is now ₹6.25 lakh.

Do the sum: ₹6,25,800 divided by ₹4.9 per km comes to about 1.28 lakh km. That is how far you now have to drive before owning the battery outright works out cheaper than renting it. Before this hike that crossover sat near 1.12 lakh km. The increase pushed it roughly 15,000 km further out.

## 1,545 in October, and a price rise straight after

The timing says something. Vahan registration data for October puts the Tomahawk EV at 1,545 units — its strongest month so far, and well up on the 443 it managed in September, its first proper month in showrooms.

That is a real ramp, but it is not a car MG is struggling to sell at the old number either. Raising prices right after your best month is what a company does when the order book is healthy enough to take it. For comparison, MG's own Windsor EV did 3,127 registrations in the same month, so the Tomahawk is now roughly half a Windsor and climbing.

## The car itself is untouched

Same 69.2 kWh LFP pack. Same single front motor, 201 bhp and 310 Nm. Same 517 km MIDC claim, same 8.2-second 0-100 km/h, same 90 kW peak on DC fast charging. V2L, V2V and V2H all carry over, and vehicle-to-home is still a rare thing on an Indian car.

## What this means for you

Already booked? Ask your dealer in writing whether your booking is locked at the old price. Do not assume it.

Still deciding? The sum has changed shape. At ₹20.24 lakh, a seven-seat electric SUV with a 517 km claim is still the cheapest way into that body style in India, so the car has not lost its main argument. But the BaaS route just got more tempting for anyone doing ordinary city distances. Drive 15,000 km a year and you would need roughly eight and a half years to reach that 1.28 lakh km crossover.

The bigger read: a price rise this soon after launch usually means the launch number did its job. MG priced the Tomahawk EV to get noticed, got noticed, and has now moved to the figure it actually wanted.
