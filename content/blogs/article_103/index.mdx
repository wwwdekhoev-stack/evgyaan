₹16,311 is a real amount of money at the bottom of the electric scooter market. That is the gap between the [Ather Konarc](https://www.atherenergy.com/konarc) S 100 at ₹99,999 and the TVS iQube 2.3 kWh at ₹1,16,310, both ex-showroom Bengaluru.

For that money the iQube gives you 14 km more claimed range. Whether that is a good trade depends almost entirely on how you charge.

## The spec sheet

|  | Ather Konarc S 100 | TVS iQube 2.3 kWh |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price (ex-showroom, Bengaluru) | ₹99,999 | ₹1,16,310 |
| Battery | 2.1 kWh | 2.3 kWh |
| Claimed range | 100 km (IDC) | 114 km |
| Top speed | 70 km/h | 68 km/h |
| Motor | 4 kW, 16 Nm | 4.4 kW peak |
| Boot space | 31 litres | — |

These two are closer than the price suggests. Similar battery, similar top speed, 14 km between the claimed range figures. The Konarc's TrueRange figure is 80 km, which is the realistic daily number.

What the Konarc adds for less money is a steel unibody, a 14-inch front wheel, a 7-inch dashboard on the S variants, and 31 litres of boot with a 2-litre front cubby.

## Service intervals are the quiet difference

Ather quotes a 10,000 km service interval on the Konarc — roughly double what a typical scooter asks for. Over three years that is fewer workshop visits and less money spent on labour.

It is not a headline feature, but it is the kind of thing that shows up in your wallet.

## Charging and DC fast charging

The Konarc's 450W charger is built into the scooter, so a plain cable replaces the usual charging brick. 0-80% takes about four hours on that alone. The optional 450W additional charger works alongside it for 900W combined, cutting that to roughly two hours.

TVS supplies a portable charger with the iQube and quotes about 2 hours 25 minutes for 10-80% on the 2.3 kWh pack, which is quick for the segment.

The difference is what happens away from home. The Konarc can use Ather Grid, which crossed over 5,000 public fast chargers nationwide as of Ather's January 2026 network update, with Ather claiming up to 13 km of range from a 10-minute stop on the S 100. The iQube has no DC fast-charging option at all.

## The catch on the Konarc

Deliveries. The S 100 is last in Ather's rollout, with the entry-level variant slated for Q1 2027, while the S 125 and S 161 started reaching buyers from mid-September 2026. The iQube is in showrooms today, and TVS is pushing [towards 80,000 units a month](https://evgyan.com/blogs/tvs-electric-scooter-capacity-expansion) of capacity.

If you need a scooter in the next two months, this comparison is academic.

## What this means for you

**If you can wait for Q1 2027 and have charging at home:** the Konarc S 100 is the better value — ₹16,311 less, a longer service interval, a bigger boot, and fast-charging access the iQube does not have.

**If you need a scooter now:** the TVS iQube 2.3 kWh is in showrooms today, and 114 km of claimed range is the more generous figure of the two.

**If you ride close to the limit of a 100 km scooter daily:** the iQube's extra range earns its premium, whichever one is actually available when you walk into the showroom.
