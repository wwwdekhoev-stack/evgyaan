Ather Energy has a problem that most companies would consider a good one to have — except it's costing them real sales every single month.

On 26 October, Ather said it is **missing out on 18,000-20,000 potential sales a month** purely because of a shortfall in manufacturing capacity, not because of weak demand.

## Reading between the lines

This is a significant admission from one of India's top electric scooter brands. A few things stand out:

- This isn't a demand problem — it's explicitly a **supply/capacity** problem. Ather is saying buyers want the scooters; it simply can't build them fast enough.
- 18,000-20,000 units a month is not a small number. Scaled over a year, that's **over 2 lakh scooters** of lost sales potential — a figure that would meaningfully move Ather's overall market share if captured.
- It comes at a time when the broader e2W (electric two-wheeler) market in India has just crossed **2 lakh units sold in a single month** for the first time (September 2026), so overall category demand is clearly there to be captured.

## Why this matters

For a company gearing up for sustained growth, admitting a capacity shortfall publicly is unusual — most companies prefer to talk about the demand side of the story, not where they're falling short operationally. It suggests Ather is either setting expectations ahead of a capacity expansion announcement, or signalling to investors and dealers that the bottleneck is temporary and fixable, not a demand ceiling.

Either way, if you've been waiting on an Ather scooter and wondering why delivery timelines feel long, this is effectively Ather confirming — in its own words — that it's a manufacturing issue, not that interest has cooled.
