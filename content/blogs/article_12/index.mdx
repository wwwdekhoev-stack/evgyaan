Ather builds about 33,000 scooters a month and gets about 60,000 pre-orders a month. That gap is why CEO Tarun Mehta told analysts on 26 October that the company is leaving roughly 20,000 retail sales a month on the table. Autocar Professional, which [first reported the Q2 FY27 call](https://www.autocarpro.in/news/ather-flags-unmet-monthly-sales-of-20000-units-plans-capacity-expansion-135476), put the range at 18,000-20,000.

This is a problem every EV maker wants to have. It is also one Ather has already started paying to fix.

## The numbers behind the gap

- Average production in Q2 FY27: about 33,000 scooters a month
- Pre-orders in the quarter: about 1.8 lakh, or roughly 60,000 a month, up about 108% year on year
- Hosur plant utilisation at peak: about 98%
- Vahan market share: 15.1% in Q2 FY27, down from 17.2% a year ago

Read those together and the share drop makes sense. Ather didn't lose buyers. It ran out of scooters to sell them.

## The ₹122 crore answer

On the same call, Ather's board approved about ₹122 crore to add roughly 2.25 lakh units of annual capacity by Q2 FY28: ₹58 crore at Hosur and ₹64 crore at the new Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar plant. Total capacity after that lands near 11.4 lakh units a year.

That is a cheap fix by industry standards. The cost per unit of added capacity works out to about ₹5,400 (₹122 crore ÷ 2.25 lakh), because Ather is adding lines inside plants it already owns rather than building new ones.

## What this means for you

If your Ather delivery date keeps slipping, this is the company telling you why. It is a factory problem, not a demand problem, and the extra capacity arrives in stages through FY28. Ask your dealer whether your variant is one of the mid-range models Ather is prioritising; the entry-level Konarc has been [pushed back](/blogs/ather-delays-konarc-entry-level) to protect margins.

## The EVGyan take

Admitting a shortfall on an analyst call is unusual, and the context matters: Ather posted its first quarterly profit in Q2, so it can afford to say demand is ahead of supply. The ₹122 crore plan is small enough that it should land on time. The test is whether the 15.1% share recovers once the new lines run. We'll check the Vahan numbers every month.
