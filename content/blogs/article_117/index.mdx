Honda is putting $3 billion into a brand new car factory, and the vehicles it was announced for are hybrids. The Honda Ohio hybrid plant will sit between the company's existing East Liberty and Marysville sites, build 240,000 vehicles a year, and add more than 2,000 jobs. Production is expected to start in 2029, once the last government approvals are in. It will be Honda's fourth car plant in Ohio and its ninth car-related facility in the US, taking American car capacity to roughly 1.3 million vehicles a year.

## The EV part is permission, not a promise

Read Honda's own announcement closely and the electric bit is a door left open rather than a plan. The company says the plant can build several vehicle types and powertrains, "including hybrid and gasoline-powered vehicles, as well as battery-electric vehicles", and that it wants to adapt output as "electrification trends evolve".

What the release does not contain is one electric model name, an EV volume, or an EV date. The hybrid side gets all three. Honda is also lifting hybrid system capacity at its Ohio transmission plant by 250%, retooling its Anna engine plant for a next-generation four-cylinder, and adding a V6 hybrid engine in Alabama. Globally it has said it will bring 15 new Honda and Acura hybrid models by 2030.

A hybrid factory that is allowed to make EVs later, then. Worth being precise, because a lot of the coverage is reading this as Honda quitting electric. The release does not say that.

## Where that leaves Honda's India EVs

India is the more interesting half. Honda has three electric scooters here: the Activa e, the QC1, and the QC3, which arrived in September 2026 at ₹1.35 lakh ex-showroom and went to five cities first.

The volumes are small. Vahan figures reported in a 22 October interview with Honda's India two-wheeler arm put the company at 940 electric two-wheeler registrations in July against a segment of 2.06 lakh. That is 0.5% share. The same month:

- TVS: 55,892 registrations
- Bajaj: 45,910
- Ather: 30,704
- Vida: 23,003
- HMSI: 940

HMSI president, MD and CEO Tsutsumu Otani said in that interview the company is first studying what used electric two-wheelers will be worth, adding: "Perhaps within the next three years we will have a clearer understanding."

Three years is a long time in a segment doing over two lakh units a month.

## What this means for you

Nothing changes today if you are shopping an Activa e, QC1 or QC3. The Activa e is still listed at about ₹1.20 lakh to ₹1.55 lakh ex-showroom across its two variants, with a claimed 102 km and batteries that go to a Honda swap station rather than a home socket.

What the Ohio money tells you is about pace. Honda's big capital is going to hybrids in a market where hybrids sell, while India's electric two-wheeler market is being built by companies that already committed. Honda has the biggest scooter dealer network in the country, and the day it points that at electric, 0.5% can move fast. Until then, the four brands above are not waiting.
