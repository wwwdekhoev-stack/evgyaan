Uber wants 5,000 more electric autos on its app in India over the next 12 months, and it has gone to Mahindra to find them.

The two companies signed an MoU at an event in Mumbai on 16 November. Mahindra Last Mile Mobility will put 5,000 of its electric three-wheelers onto Uber's platform through a programme the two are calling EV Auto Advantage. The focus, both sides say, will be cities where EV uptake is still slow rather than the metros that already run on electric autos.

Here is the interesting bit. The vehicle was never the hard part.

## The loan is the product

Mahindra already sells electric autos, and rivals are busy too, from Bajaj to [Montra's fast-charging Super Auto](/blogs/montra-super-auto-fx-15-minute-charging). India already buys them in big numbers. What stops an individual driver from switching is the down payment, the loan rate and the fear of what the thing will be worth in four years. EV Auto Advantage is aimed at exactly that gap: the companies say it will help drivers with funding, give them something for the vehicle they trade in, and support them through the switch.

What they have not said is the detail that actually decides whether a driver signs. No interest rate, no down payment, no tenure, no buyback number, no list of cities. Until those land, this is a plan rather than an offer.

Suman Mishra, Managing Director and CEO of Mahindra Last Mile Mobility, framed it the right way: "Sustainable mobility will scale when it works for the driver first."

## 5,000 is a small number. Look at what it sits against.

India registered 47,159 electric passenger autos (L5) in October 2026 alone, as per Vahan. Spread across a 31-day month, that is roughly 1,520 a day. So Uber and Mahindra's full-year target is just over three days of what the segment already does.

That is not a knock on the deal. It is the opposite. The three-wheeler is the one segment in India where the electric argument is already over:

- Electric three-wheeler registrations in October 2026: 88,562, up 24.8% in a year
- Electric passenger autos (L5) alone: 47,159, up 95.3%
- Share of all new three-wheelers that were electric: 62.6%

Nothing else on Indian roads is close to that. Mahindra is No. 2 in electric three-wheelers with 15,154 registrations in October, just 571 behind Bajaj, and says it has more than 4 lakh commercial EVs running in India. Our [October electric three-wheeler report](/blogs/electric-three-wheeler-sales-october-2026) has the full brand table.

## The EVGyan take

When more than 60% of new three-wheelers are already electric, the remaining job is not convincing anyone that electric works. It is paperwork. Getting a first-time driver past a bank's credit check and giving him a trade-in price he trusts is slower, duller work than launching a new model, and it is the work that is actually left.

So treat the 5,000 as a pilot, not a headline. If the financing terms turn out to be genuinely better than what a driver gets at his local dealership, the number gets much bigger next year. If they are the same terms with a new name on them, it stays at 5,000.

One footnote worth watching: Mahindra Last Mile Mobility is among the finalists for this year's Earthshot Prize, in its Clean Our Air category. That winner is announced in Mumbai on 17 November.
