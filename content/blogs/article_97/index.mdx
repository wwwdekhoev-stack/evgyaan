Tesla has added 27 miles of range to its cheapest Model Y and asked nobody for an extra rupee. The rear-wheel-drive Model Y Standard now reads 348 miles, about 560 km, on Tesla's US order page. It read 321 miles (516 km) before. The price has not moved from $39,990.

Work the Tesla Model Y range increase out and it comes to 8.4% more distance on the same money: 27 divided by 321. There is no new battery behind it, no new motor, and no press release.

## Tesla hasn't said what changed

The rest of the spec sheet is untouched. Same 6.8 seconds to 60 mph, same 125 mph top speed, same nickel-based pack of roughly 69.5 kWh built at Fremont or Giga Texas, according to Drive Tesla Canada. On what produced the extra miles, Drive Tesla Canada's line is blunt: "Tesla has not explained what accounts for the additional range."

One caveat before you treat 348 as gospel. As of Sunday, the EPA's own fueleconomy.gov listing still showed the car at 321 miles. The higher figure is Tesla's configurator talking, not the federal database.

The timing says something too. The bump landed days after Tesla pulled the entry-level Model Y AWD, a 294-mile car at $41,990, off its order page. The cheaper car with more range stayed. The costlier one with less range went.

## Canada kept its old number

Canadian buyers still see 463 km, and that is not an oversight. Their Model Y rolls out of Giga Berlin carrying an LFP pack of 63 kWh, so it is both a different chemistry and a smaller one. Same badge on the boot, different cells inside, different rating.

## What this means for you

Nothing on an Indian price list changes today. Tesla sells the Model Y Premium RWD here at ₹50.89 lakh ex-showroom with a claimed 500 km and a 60 kWh pack, and the six-seat Model Y L at ₹61.99 lakh with 82 kWh and a claimed 681 km. Different spec, different test cycle, so the American figure does not carry over.

What does carry over is the habit. A range number is not fixed on launch day. It moves when the trim mix, the software or the test changes, and the carmaker is under no obligation to tell you it moved. If you are shortlisting an EV, read the figure on the day you book, not the one in a six-month-old review.

## The EVGyan take

Free range is good news and we will not pretend otherwise. But 348 miles is a test result, not a guarantee, and the gap between a lab figure and a Delhi-Jaipur run in December is the number that actually matters. Tesla's own Indian cars are rated 500 km and 681 km. Ask any owner what they see at 100 km/h with the AC on, and you will get a third set of numbers.
