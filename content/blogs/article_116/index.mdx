Force Motors has put a date on its first electric vans, and a number behind the plan: ₹3,000 crore. Managing director Prasan Firodia says the Force Traveller EV will launch between January and March 2027, with an electric ambulance among the first versions to reach customers.

The Force Traveller EV launch has been a long time coming. Force showed an electric Traveller at the 2024 Auto Expo and then went quiet on sales. Firodia told PTI the vehicles are now fully developed and sitting with ARAI for homologation and final testing — the last box to tick before a commercial launch in India.

## What the ₹3,000 crore is actually for

The money is not an EV-only cheque. Firodia said the ₹3,000 crore is split between factory capacity, engineering work, electrification, hybrid drivelines and digitisation. Alongside it, Force is taking its production capacity to 85,000 vehicles a year.

That last figure matters more than it sounds. Force is a small company in a market where Tata and Mahindra count in lakhs. Building electric vans for ambulances, school runs and staff transport is a segment the big two have barely touched, and Force already owns the Traveller name there.

An electric Urbania is next, still under development and a few months from even starting homologation.

## The honest part: this will be slow

Firodia was blunt about the pace. He does not expect the shift in passenger carriers under seven metres to look anything like what has happened in scooters, three-wheelers or cars. Fleet buyers run the maths before they run the vehicle, and range limits plus patchy charging still break that maths on long or changing routes.

He sees school transport moving first, because the route repeats every day and the daily distance is known to the kilometre. You can size a battery for that. Tourism and intercity work, where a van might do 180 km one day and 600 km the next, is the hard case.

Rushlane reports the e-Traveller Smart Citibus EV shown earlier carries a 70 kWh battery and a 14+D seating layout. Force has not confirmed that as the launch specification, and no prices have been announced.

## What this means for you

If you buy or hire vans — a school, a hospital, a factory bus contract — 2027 is the year you finally get a diesel Traveller alternative from the company that built your diesel Traveller. That is a bigger deal than one more electric SUV launch, because nobody has to retrain a driver or rebuild a service relationship.

## The EVGyan take

A ₹3,000 crore spend that openly includes hybrid driveline technology tells you Force is hedging, not betting the company. We would rather have that than a press release promising a full electric lineup by a date nobody keeps. An ambulance is also the smartest possible first product: short city runs, a predictable daily distance, and a buyer who cares about cost per kilometre, not 0-100 times.

Watch for the price. Force has not said a word about it yet, and in the fleet business that is the only number that decides anything.
