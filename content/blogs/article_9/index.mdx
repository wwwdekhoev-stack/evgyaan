China dominates almost every layer of the global EV supply chain — from battery minerals to finished cars. Hyundai's India boss thinks there's a realistic challenger to that dominance, and it isn't any single country.

On 27 October, **Tarun Garg**, CEO of Hyundai Motor India, said that India and South Korea, working together, can position themselves as an alternative to China in the EV space.

## Why this pairing makes sense

The logic isn't hard to follow:

- South Korea already has deep EV manufacturing expertise and some of the world's biggest battery makers (LG Energy Solution, Samsung SDI, SK On)
- India has scale — one of the largest pools of potential EV buyers in the world — along with a growing push for local manufacturing and a government keen to de-risk EV supply chains away from China
- Hyundai itself sits at the intersection of both markets, with deep roots in Korea and a major, newly-listed manufacturing base in India

## What it would take

Garg's comment is more of a direction-setter than an announcement of anything concrete. For India and South Korea to genuinely become a China alternative, a few things need to fall in place — Indian battery cell manufacturing scaling up, more component localisation, and Korean OEMs treating India as an export base rather than just a sales market.

It also fits the broader mood right now: automakers globally are actively looking for ways to reduce how dependent they are on Chinese supply chains for EV components, especially batteries and rare-earth magnets. Whether India-Korea can actually scale up to fill that gap is the real test — but having a company as large as Hyundai talk about it publicly is a meaningful signal of intent.
