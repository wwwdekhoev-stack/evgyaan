If you booked a Tata EV recently and the dealer keeps saying "next month", here is a number to hold on to: 2 to 3 months. That is how long Tata Motors says it needs before its electric car supply catches up with bookings that have tripled since February 2026.

Shailesh Chandra, MD of Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles and Tata Passenger Electric Mobility, gave that timeline at the Autocar Professional India EV Conclave in New Delhi on 27 October, as [reported by Autocar Professional](https://www.autocarpro.in/news/tata-motors-expects-ev-supply-to-catch-up-with-demand-in-23-months-shailesh-chandra-135489).

## Why bookings jumped 3x

Chandra's explanation is not the one most of us would have guessed. The main trigger, he said, was the Middle East crisis: rising crude prices and the fear of fuel shortages pushed a lot of fence-sitters to book an EV. New launches helped, but the fuel scare did the heavy lifting.

That matters because nobody planned for it. Carmakers and suppliers lock production plans 18 to 24 months ahead. Tata can move some capacity between petrol and electric lines, but cell, power-electronics and semiconductor suppliers need new equipment, and that takes a few months to arrive.

## How big the backlog is

Tata dispatched 47,150 electric cars in July-September 2026, about 24% of everything it sold that quarter, and 1,08,548 EVs in the first nine months of the year. Bookings at 3x the February pace means the order book is running well ahead of output, which averaged about 15,700 cars a month in that quarter (47,150 ÷ 3).

The dealer-stock numbers tell the same story. Chandra said Tata EVs are sitting at under 10 days of inventory, against 35-40 days for petrol and diesel cars. His read: real EV demand has already crossed 10% of the market, even if registered penetration (8.45% in September, per FADA) hasn't caught up yet.

## What this means for you

- Waiting periods should start easing from around January 2027 if Chandra's 2-3 month estimate holds.
- Popular variants of the Nexon EV, Punch EV and Harrier EV will clear last. If you are flexible on colour, ask the dealer what is in transit.
- Don't expect discounts while stock is this tight. The festive offers will be on petrol cars, where inventory is 4x higher.

## The EVGyan take

A leader publicly admitting it can't build cars fast enough is a better sign for Indian EVs than any launch. Demand that was supposed to be about subsidies turned out to be about fuel bills, and that kind of demand doesn't switch off when the crisis cools. We'll track Tata's monthly registrations to see how fast the backlog actually clears.
