Tata Motors has a good problem on its hands — too many people want its electric cars, faster than it can build them.

Speaking on 27 October, **Shailesh Chandra**, Managing Director of Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles and Tata Passenger Electric Mobility, said the company's EV supply will catch up with demand within the **next 2-3 months**.

## Why supply fell behind

According to Chandra, EV bookings at Tata have **tripled since February 2026**. That's a sharp jump by any measure, and it's clearly caught even Tata's own production planning off guard.

A few things are likely feeding this:

- The **Harrier EV** and **Curvv EV** have both found strong footing since their launches
- Price actions and new variants across the **Nexon EV**, **Tiago EV** and **Punch EV** range have pulled in buyers who were on the fence
- Tata still commands the single largest share of India's electric car market, so even a modest rise in overall EV interest shows up as a large absolute jump in Tata's order book

## What this means for buyers

If you've recently booked a Tata EV and you're staring at a long waiting period, Chandra's comment is at least a timeline to hold onto — **2-3 months** rather than an open-ended wait. For a market leader to publicly call out a demand-supply gap, rather than just quietly extending delivery dates, is also a signal that Tata expects this phase to be temporary rather than structural.

It also tells us something about the health of India's EV car market heading into the festive season and year-end: demand isn't just holding up, it's accelerating faster than production lines can be ramped.

We'll keep tracking Tata's monthly numbers to see exactly how fast that backlog clears.
