JLR will bring its first electric cars to India in 2027, and the trade deal that just made its petrol and diesel models far cheaper gives those EVs nothing for five years. The Range Rover Electric India launch, along with the Range Rover Sport Electric, was confirmed by JLR India managing director and CEO Rajan Amba in an interview with PTI on 10 November.

Both will be imported at first, to test how the market responds rather than commit to local assembly from day one. JLR India has no electric car in its range today.

## The five-year catch nobody is talking about

Here is the part that decides what you will pay. The India-UK trade agreement has been in force since 15 July. On qualifying petrol and diesel cars built in Britain, it pulls import duty down from a rate that ran as high as 110%. Electric, hybrid and hydrogen cars get no concession at all for the first five years, according to an October report on the trade agreement, and Amba's line then was that JLR is not counting on that relief arriving any sooner.

So the sequence is awkward. The combustion Range Rovers got their price cut in 2026; the electric ones arrive in 2027 and wait until the back half of the decade for theirs.

The size of what they are missing is easy to see. Amba told PTI one model's price has already come down from around ₹4.25 crore to about ₹3.5 crore after the revision. That is roughly ₹75 lakh off one car (₹4.25 crore minus ₹3.5 crore), and it is the kind of cut the electric Range Rovers will not get.

## What else is coming

An earlier October report said JLR India is lining up four electric models for early 2027, naming the Range Rover Electric, the Range Rover Sport Electric, an all-electric Range Rover GT and the Jaguar Type 01. PTI's interview names only the two Range Rovers plus a new Jaguar GT next year, and gives no count, so treat the fourth model as unconfirmed for now.

The rest of the business is in better shape than the EV timeline suggests:

- Retail outlets: 40 before 2026 closes, up from 25 a year ago
- Defender orders: expected to rise around 20% from current levels on localisation
- Defender, Range Rover and Range Rover Sport: more than 80% of JLR India sales
- Average transaction price: roughly ₹1.4 crore

## What this means for you

If you were hoping an electric Range Rover would land near its diesel sibling's new post-FTA price, that is not the 2027 story. Imported, full duty, no concession — expect it to sit above the combustion cars, not below them.

The fix is the one JLR has already proved works. Localisation is exactly why Defender orders are expected to rise around 20%, and the same lever is sitting there for the EVs. Nothing in the trade deal stops JLR assembling an electric Range Rover here and pricing it properly; the five-year wait applies to importing one. "Electric is the way forward," Amba told PTI, and the company is spending £18 billion globally through FY29 to get there. So 2027 is a test, and the number worth watching is what JLR decides about assembly once it sees how that test goes.
