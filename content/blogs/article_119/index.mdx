A Mercedes EQS 450+ just drove 867 km on one charge. Not on a claimed figure, not on an ARAI sticker — on a real test route, with the air-conditioning on.

The Mercedes EQS range number comes from the Edmunds EV Range Test in the United States, and it is the longest any electric sedan has managed in that test. In miles, which is how the test is reported, it is 539. The car's official EPA rating is 752 km, so it beat its own certified figure by 116 km.

Only one EV in that test has gone further. The Cadillac Escalade IQ managed 898 km, and it needs a 205 kWh battery to do it. The EQS did its 867 km on 122 kWh.

## Why the efficiency matters more than the range

867 km on 122 kWh works out to roughly 18.4 kWh per 100 km in the test, against an EPA estimate of 17.4. The 2025 EQS 450+ did 747 km on the same route and used 21.1. So the new one went 120 km further while drinking less, on an 800-volt architecture and a bigger pack rather than a bigger motor.

Charging improved by more than the range did:

- Peak: 350 kW, up from 208 kW on the old EQS
- Session average: 222 kW, up from 175 kW
- Range added in an hour: 1,328 km
- Range added in just over 7 minutes: 161 km

Roughly twice as quick as the 2025 car.

## The India connection you can actually use

The EQS 450+ is not on sale in India. But the same Edmunds test run gives us a number that is directly relevant: the 2027 Mercedes GLC Electric managed 631 km.

That is the car Mercedes launched in India this month at ₹78.50 lakh. Its India claim is an ARAI-cycle figure, and ARAI numbers are generous. A 631 km result on a 60 percent city, 40 percent highway route at an average 64 kmph is a different kind of evidence — the sort you can plan a Mumbai-Pune-Mumbai run around.

From the same test batch: the BMW iX3 50 xDrive did 702 km, the Volvo EX60 566 km.

## What this means for you

The lesson is not "buy an EQS". It is that the gap between a certified range figure and what a car actually does has started going the right way. An EV used to fall short of its sticker. This one beat it by 116 km.

The number to ask a showroom for is not the claimed range. It is kWh per 100 km. At 18.4, a 60 kWh Indian EV would do about 326 km for real — and that is the maths that decides whether you charge at home twice a week or hunt for a charger on a Sunday.

## The EVGyan take

A luxury sedan nobody in India can buy setting a range record sounds like news for somebody else. It is not. Mercedes put the same generation of electric hardware into the GLC Electric it is selling here, and that car just did 631 km on the toughest independent range test going. Big batteries are easy. Efficiency is the hard part, and Mercedes has clearly worked on it.
