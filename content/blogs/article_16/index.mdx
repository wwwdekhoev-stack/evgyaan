Two separate stories this week show India's electric public transport and last-mile mobility push expanding well beyond the usual metro-city headlines.

## 220 electric buses, a 12-year commitment, three Gujarat cities

On 26 October, **Jakson Green** said it had won a CESL tender to supply and operate **220 electric buses** across **three Gujarat cities** under the PM-eBus Sewa scheme, on a **12-year Gross Cost Contract**: 120 buses for Vadodara, 80 for Gandhidham and 20 for Navsari, all 9-metre AC buses (company statement, via [Autocar Professional](https://www.autocarpro.in/news/jakson-green-e-mobility-secures-order-for-220-electric-buses-in-gujarat-135470)). Long-tenure contracts like this matter in electric bus deployment — a 12-year commitment gives the operator real certainty to plan charging infrastructure, maintenance and battery replacement cycles around, rather than scrambling year to year.

## Montra Electric bets on Assam's e3W boom

Separately, on 28 October, **Montra Electric** said it had opened a new dealership in **Guwahati** (as reported by Autocar Professional), specifically to serve what it's calling Assam's **growing electric three-wheeler market**. Electric three-wheelers (e-rickshaws and last-mile cargo autos) have quietly become one of the fastest-adopting EV categories in India, especially in Tier-2 towns and the Northeast, where they're often a primary earning vehicle for drivers rather than a discretionary purchase.

## Why the geography here matters

Both stories point to the same broader trend: India's EV growth story is no longer just a Delhi-Mumbai-Bengaluru phenomenon. A 12-year bus contract in Gujarat and a dedicated e3W dealership in Guwahati both reflect manufacturers and operators betting on EV demand well outside the usual metro clusters — in public transport and in commercial last-mile categories where the economics of running electric, rather than petrol or diesel, already clearly make sense.
