Park the scooter. Walk away. That was the entire charging routine on Hero's EICMA 2026 stand in Milan, where a wireless charging pad for the Vida VX2 platform sat beside the 140 km scooter it is built for. No cable, no socket, no plug.

To be clear: this is a technology showcase, not a product with a price or a date. Hero displayed it alongside the Vida Energy Dock, which turns the scooter's battery into a power source for other things. Both are ideas the company is working on, not things it sells.

## How the pad works

Same trick as the wireless charger on your bedside table, scaled up. One coil sits in a flat pad on the floor. A second goes into a receiver fitted low on the scooter, under the floorboard. Roll on top, the coils line up, power crosses the gap by induction. Hero's demo film walks through the handshake in steps: vehicle detected, coupling established, power flowing.

Hero has published no power rating, no efficiency figure and no charging time, and nobody else has them either. That gap matters. Inductive transfer always loses some energy as heat compared with a cable, and how much it loses decides whether this is clever or merely neat.

## Ola got here first

Ola Electric showed its Flux Charger concept at its Sankalp customer event, built the same way: one coil housed beneath the scooter, fitted at the factory, and another inside a floor platform. Then it went quiet on output, speed and timing. Two of India's biggest scooter makers have now shown the same idea, and neither has committed to building it.

## Why Europe suits it better

The VX2 sells in 10 European countries, so the pad has an obvious first home. Buyers there park in private garages and reserved bays, on flat clean floors that belong to them. A pad bolted down stays where you left it.

Indian parking is a different animal. A pad living on the ground has to survive a shared society basement, monsoon water and the daily scrape of kerbs. The bigger worry is the receiver on the scooter. It sits low, in the zone Indian roads punish hardest, and a scooter that bottoms out on a bad speed breaker would be putting a charging component in the firing line. The convenience is real. So is the repair bill.

Vida has also already solved home charging here another way. The VX2 runs removable 2.2 kWh packs you unclip and carry up to your flat. A floor pad asks you to own the floor first, and plenty of Indian riders do not.

## What this means for you

Nothing to buy, nothing to wait for. What is worth noting is what Hero now takes to a show like this. For years Indian brands went to Milan with models; this year Hero went with technology, and that is how a feature begins its walk to a showroom in Pune. Watch for the first power and efficiency figures. Those decide whether it becomes a product or stays a very good-looking idea.
