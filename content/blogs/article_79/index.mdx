Maharashtra wants almost one in three new vehicles sold in the state to run on electricity by 2030. Chief Minister Devendra Fadnavis repeated that promise this week, at an event built around a very different kind of EV: Mercedes-Benz's new GLC Electric.

## A policy speech at a car launch

Mercedes-Benz launched the GLC Electric, its first SUV in India built on the brand's new EV-only platform, at its Chakan plant near Pune on 4 November. The car is a big deal on its own: locally assembled, priced at ₹78.50 lakh ex-showroom (Delhi), and [already covered in detail on EVGyan](/blogs/mercedes-glc-electric-price-india).

But Fadnavis used the stage for something bigger. He told the gathering that Maharashtra has set a target of 30% of new vehicle registrations being electric by 2030, the same number the state wrote into its EV Policy back in 2025, now repeated at a global carmaker's own event.

## The money behind the push

The CM put a number on Mercedes-Benz's bet on the state too: ₹3,200 crore invested in Maharashtra so far, supporting around 1,200 direct jobs at Chakan and roughly 18,000 more across the supplier and service chain around it. He framed it as the state moving from "Make in Maharashtra" to "Innovate in Maharashtra," and asked Mercedes-Benz to use Chakan to also build for other Asian markets, not just India.

Maharashtra, he said, already makes more than one in five vehicles built in India. Deputy CM Sunetra Pawar, speaking alongside him, credited Mercedes-Benz's three decades in the state as proof the relationship works both ways.

## Is 30% a stretch?

It's a big number for a state still building out charging infrastructure on its own highways. Fadnavis listed some of the fixes already underway as the groundwork for getting there: fast chargers along the Mumbai-Pune Expressway and the Samruddhi Mahamarg, plus battery recycling plants.

## The EVGyan take

A premium carmaker doubling down on local EV manufacturing, with a state government willing to put a number and a deadline on its own ambition, is a good sign regardless of how the GLC Electric's price tag sits with most buyers. Mercedes-Benz said it had more than 650 expressions of interest within 36 hours of opening bookings in early October. A small sample, but a real one, of who is actually buying electric at the top end of the market right now.
