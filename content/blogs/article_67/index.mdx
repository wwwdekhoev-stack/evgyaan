The Tesla Model Y sold 164,871 units worldwide in September 2026, an all-time record for a single model and about 2.5 times what the runner-up managed. Here are the world's top 10 best-selling EVs for the month, with the numbers.

## The top 10 EVs in the world, September 2026

Figures are global monthly sales. Where a model comes in both battery-electric and plug-in hybrid versions, the split is shown.

1. **Tesla Model Y:** 164,871
2. **Tesla Model 3:** 64,680
3. **Geely Xingyuan** (sold abroad as EX2 or E2): 54,830
4. **BYD Song / Seal U:** 54,519 (18,791 electric + 35,728 plug-in hybrid)
5. **BYD Yuan Up / Atto 2:** 40,468 (30,372 electric + 10,096 plug-in hybrid)
6. **BYD Seagull / Dolphin Mini:** 32,257
7. **Fang Cheng Bao Ti 7** (BYD's premium brand): 28,225 (15,000 electric + 13,225 plug-in hybrid)
8. **BYD Dolphin:** 25,452
9. **MG 4:** 23,082 (a record for the car)
10. **Leapmotor A10 / B03X:** 22,789

Together these 10 cars sold 511,173 units in a single month.

## What stands out

The Model Y is 100,191 units clear of the Model 3, and the two are moving in opposite directions. The Model Y grew 17% on last September, while the Model 3 slipped 4%.

The tightest race is for 3rd place. Just 311 units separate the Geely Xingyuan (54,830) from the BYD Song (54,519), and the Song grew 19% year on year, the fastest in the top 5.

Brand-wise, BYD Group fills 5 of the 10 places and sold 180,921 units across them. Tesla has only 2 entries, but they add up to 229,551.

## What this means for you

The EVGyan take: Tesla owns the top of the chart, but the volume is in small, affordable cars. Four of the 10 are compact B-segment hatchbacks and crossovers (Xingyuan, Yuan Up, Seagull and A10), and together they sold 150,344 units. Expect the next big wave of EVs to follow the same recipe: small footprint, low price, enough range for the city.

One honest caveat: this is not a pure battery ranking. The Song, for example, is about two-thirds plug-in hybrid (35,728 of 54,519). The top two spots, though, are all-electric.

All of this sits inside a market of around 2.16 million plug-in vehicles in the month, up 3% on last year, with battery-electric cars alone setting a record of about 1.54 million.
