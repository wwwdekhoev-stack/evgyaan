Blackbuck EV has fitted 42 sleeper berths and as much as 565 kWh of battery into one 15-metre electric coach.

The ELON 3X, revealed on 3 November, is built for overnight intercity runs. That is the bus most Indians actually take between cities, and it is the one segment electric buses have so far left alone. The Blackbuck EV ELON 3X offers two battery options, 425 kWh and 565 kWh, on a three-axle chassis with active tag-axle steering. R K Raju, founder and CEO, says they started from an electric platform instead of taking a diesel bus and swapping the engine out.

## What is on the spec sheet, and what is missing

- Length: 15 metres, three axles
- Berths: 42 for passengers, with separate accommodation for two crew
- Battery: 425 kWh or 565 kWh
- Luggage: more than 9 cubic metres
- Platform: Born-Electric Skateboard Architecture
- Suspension: independent, with electronically controlled air suspension
- Safety: EBS, ABS, electronic stability control, disc brakes on all wheels

Blackbuck says four years went into engineering the coach, two of them testing on real roads. Reservations are open and commercial production is targeted for mid-2027.

What the company has not published: range, charging time, motor output and price. For an operator running a bus overnight between two cities, those four figures are the entire decision.

## The bus got bigger. The deadline got later

This part is not in today's coverage. Back in August 2024, Blackbuck showed the ELON 3X sleeper at 13.5 metres with 36 berths, 8 cubic metres of luggage and a claimed 300 km or 400 km per charge. It said then that its first micro-factory would be running by Q3 2025, and that it would be at full scale before 2025 ended.

Two years later the bus is 1.5 metres longer, carries six more berths and a cubic metre more luggage. Production has moved to mid-2027, roughly 18 months past that original date.

None of that is strange for a startup building a 15-metre vehicle from a blank sheet, and the newer bus is plainly the better one. It is still worth knowing before anyone counts on seeing one on a highway next year.

## The EVGyan take

India registered 768 electric buses in October 2026 by our own Vahan count, and nearly all of them were city buses bought on government contracts. Intercity sleeper coaches are different: private operators buy them, and they run long distances through the night. Harder job, bigger prize.

The thing to watch here is not the battery size. It is the charging. A 565 kWh pack needs a depot that can push a lot of power into it in a few hours, and private bus operators do not have those depots today. Whoever fixes the depot wins the route.
