Green SM has crossed 15 lakh rides in India in roughly five months, and it now wants to run its cabs across six states and territories.

The Green SM electric taxi service, run by a Vietnamese operator from the same group as VinFast, started in Delhi-NCR on 5 June 2026 with about 1,000 cars. Hyderabad came after that. Bengaluru and Chennai are next on the list.

## Four licences down, two markets to go

Green SM says it already holds aggregator licences in four states: Delhi, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh and Karnataka. Three of those sit inside the Delhi-NCR belt it is already running in. The fourth, granted under Karnataka's 2016 aggregator rules, is what opens up Bengaluru.

No launch date has been given for Bengaluru or Chennai yet. The company says talks with the transport departments and its local partners are still on.

The Bengaluru entry has not been welcomed by everyone. Local driver bodies, including the Karnataka Rajya Chalakara Parishath, have asked the state to review the approval. That is a familiar pattern in the city. Ola, Uber and Rapido have all faced the same demand at some point.

## The Rs 8 per km confusion

Several reports said Green SM charges Rs 8 a kilometre. The company has called those reports inaccurate and unfounded. It says fares are set as per state and city transport rules, government-notified slabs included, and that you see the price in the app before booking. Pricing changes by city and by distance slab.

## What this means for you

If you are in Bengaluru or Chennai, you get another all-electric cab option, and that is something the market lost when BluSmart stopped running. The car is the VinFast Limo Green, a seven-seater electric MPV built for fleet duty, so there is a lot more room than in the usual sedan cab.

The fleet model is worth watching too. Green SM owns and operates its cars instead of signing up private drivers the way Ola and Uber do. That usually means cleaner cars and trained drivers, but it also means the company carries the full cost of every vehicle on its own books.

## The EVGyan take

Vietnam is where this model was proved. Green SM put more than 30,000 electric taxis on the road there and took roughly 40% of that taxi market. India is a harder test. Fares are lower, competition is deeper, and BluSmart already showed how fast an owned fleet can run out of money.

Even so, four licences in hand and six states on the map is real movement for a company that is five months old in this market. Bengaluru will be the proper test. If the numbers work in a cab market that price-sensitive, the rest of India gets a lot easier.
