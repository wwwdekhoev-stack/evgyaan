A company that turns ordinary cycles and pushcarts into electric ones is about to build four times as many of them. Accelero Vehicles has raised close to USD 1 million from Next Bharat Ventures, and the Accelero eBik funding takes its factory from 3,000 units a year to 12,000.

That is a small cheque by EV standards. But look at who is riding these things.

## Not the customer you expect

About 95% of eBik users work in the informal economy. Delivery riders. Street vendors. Farmers. Waste pickers. People for whom the vehicle is not a lifestyle choice but a tool that decides how much they earn by Friday.

Those figures come from NITI Aayog's Frontier Tech platform, which profiled the company in March 2026. That study counted more than 5,000 riders spread across 25-plus cities, and found 49% had given up their old vehicle entirely. Another 38% reported a direct gain in their earnings.

Accelero, set up in 2017 by Anant Jain, does not sell one product. It makes electric conversion kits for pushcarts, kits that turn a normal bicycle into an electric one, cargo set-ups, and a patented roadster e-bicycle of its own.

## Where the money goes

- Funding: about USD 1 million from Next Bharat Ventures
- Capacity: 3,000 to 12,000 units a year
- Target: 20,000 customers over the next year
- Expansion: new cities across more than 5 states
- Network: 100-plus channel partners to be added

Next Bharat Ventures is an impact fund set up in 2024 out of GIFT City, running a ₹340 crore first fund and a ₹2,000 crore second one across 21 companies. Founder and CEO Vipul Nath Jindal said the fund sees eBik as "a category-defining brand for the next billion". Earlier backers include Acumen, Social Alpha, SELCO and LetsVenture.

## The EVGyan take

Everybody in Indian EV coverage, us included, spends most of the day on the showroom end of the market. New scooters, new SUVs, new launches. That is the glamour end. It is also not where most of India's road transport actually sits.

A vegetable vendor pushing a cart eight hours a day is not cross-shopping an Ather Rizta. A retrofit kit on the cart he already owns is a different proposition altogether, because he does not have to replace the asset, finance a new one, or learn anything new. He keeps his cart. It just stops being so heavy.

That is the quiet argument for this corner of the market. The cheapest electric vehicle is often the one somebody already owns, converted.

12,000 units a year is still tiny. India registers more than 2 lakh electric two-wheelers in a single month on Vahan. Accelero's whole annual output after this expansion would be a rounding error against that. Nobody should pretend this is a scale business yet.

But scale is not the only thing worth watching. If 49% of users genuinely abandon their old vehicle, the switching rate here is better than most of what the subsidy-heavy end of the market manages.

## What this means for you

If you run a delivery cart or a small cargo operation, retrofit kits are worth pricing before you commit to a new electric three-wheeler. Accelero says it is entering new cities across five-plus states and adding over 100 channel partners this year, so availability should improve.
