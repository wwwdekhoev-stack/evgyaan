A clutch of stories from 26-28 October paint a fuller picture of where India's electric two-wheeler market actually stands — how concentrated it is, how fast buying intent is rising, and which companies are repositioning to compete.

## Just 5 brands control 83% of the market

A **BCG report** released on 26 October found that just **5 brands** account for **83%** of India's electric two-wheeler market. In a category with dozens of registered players, that's a striking level of concentration — it tells you that scale, service network and brand trust are doing most of the work in deciding who wins, not just product specs.

## Buying intent has jumped from 30% to 43%

The same BCG research found that the **intent to buy** an electric two-wheeler has risen from **30% in 2024 to 43%** now. Actual **penetration**, meanwhile, stood at **9.9%** as of August. That gap between intent (43%) and actual penetration (under 10%) is the single most important number in the report — it's the clearest sign yet of how much room the e2W category still has to grow if that intent converts into real purchases.

## Yamaha finally commits

On 27 October, **Yamaha** said it is now investing seriously in India's e2W market, after what the company itself described as **decades of unsuccessful attempts** to crack the segment. For a legacy two-wheeler giant to publicly frame its own past EV efforts as failures is unusually candid — and it signals Yamaha sees this as a now-or-never moment to not get left behind.

## Ather and Ultraviolette disagree on electric motorcycles

Also on 27 October, Ather's Chief Business Officer said electric **scooters** have gone mainstream, but electric **motorcycles** remain a tough sell. Ultraviolette's CTO took the opposite view, saying electric motorcycles could see scooter-like growth within **3 years**. Two companies building in the same space, reading the same market very differently — worth watching who turns out right.

## Bajaj picks up a ₹66.82 crore state incentive

Separately, on 28 October, the **Maharashtra government** approved an additional **₹66.82 crore** in funding for Bajaj under its 2021 EV policy — a reminder that state-level incentives are still actively shaping where EV manufacturing investment lands in India.

## What it all adds up to

Put together: the market is concentrated at the top, intent to buy is rising sharply, a reluctant legacy player (Yamaha) is finally jumping in, two serious EV-native companies disagree on the next frontier (motorcycles), and state governments are still writing cheques to keep manufacturers invested locally. That's a market still very much in motion — not one that's settled yet.
