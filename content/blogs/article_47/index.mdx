A year ago, Hero MotoCorp showed EICMA a concept sketch of a three-wheeled EV that could lean into corners like a motorcycle. At EICMA 2026 in Milan, that concept came back as a working prototype: the Next 2, built by Hero's in-house innovation hub, Novus Labs.

Hero describes the Next 2 as "a compact, flexible, space-efficient, self-balancing electric trike that combines the thrill and agility of a motorcycle with the stability, safety, and utility of three wheels." Company leadership was clear this is Hero's own ground-up take on the leaning-trike format, not a copy of what's already on sale elsewhere.

## From sketch to prototype in one year

The headline number here isn't a spec, it's a timeline. In Hero's own words on the EICMA stage: "From an idea brought to life as a concept, today it is a working prototype already moving into its next phase of development." That's a real milestone. Plenty of concepts shown at motor shows never get built at all, let alone move into a second development phase inside 12 months.

Design-wise, the Next 2 lines up with design patents Hero filed in India earlier this year for a self-balancing electric trike: twin front wheels on a complex suspension setup that lets it lean like a two-wheeler, a belt drive to the rear wheel, disc brakes on both front wheels, a large windscreen and a round digital display. Hero hasn't confirmed battery size, range, top speed, price or a launch date. Novus Labs' own mandate is to build and test ideas before deciding whether they go to Hero, to Vida, or to an entirely new venture.

## What this means for you

Don't read "working prototype" as "coming to a showroom soon." Hero was explicit that Novus Labs exists to explore ideas without a guaranteed production outcome; only once something clears that bar does it get a brand and a launch date. But a trike that promises motorcycle-like cornering with the stability of three wheels fills a real gap for riders nervous about balancing a two-wheeler in city traffic, without the bulk of a scooter-style three-wheeler.

**The EVGyan take:** self-balancing, leaning trikes have stayed a niche idea globally for over a decade. Yamaha's Niken and Piaggio's MP3 line prove there's a market, just not yet a mass one. If Hero gets the Next 2's cost and weight right, it has a genuine shot at becoming India's first mainstream leaning EV trike. For now, it's a prototype worth watching, not a vehicle worth waiting for.
