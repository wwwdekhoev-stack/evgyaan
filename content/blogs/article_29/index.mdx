Nine out of every ten electric cars India exports are built by Maruti Suzuki. The company has now crossed 50,000 cumulative EV exports since it started shipping in August 2025, and it accounted for nearly 93% of India's electric passenger vehicle exports between April and September 2026, going by SIAM data the company cited.

All of it is one car: the e Vitara. All of it comes out of one plant: Hansalpur in Gujarat.

## One factory, nearly 50 countries

Maruti ships the e Vitara to close to 50 markets. Europe took the first batch and is still the biggest destination. The company was India's largest EV exporter in FY2025-26 and has held that spot through the first half of FY2026-27.

The Hansalpur plant is doing a lot of work here. It builds every EV Maruti exports, and in FY2025-26 it handled close to half of the company's overall exports.

## The part nobody expected

Here is the odd bit. Maruti is not the name that comes up when people talk about EVs in India. That conversation is about Tata, Mahindra and MG, and the domestic registration charts back that up.

Exports are a different scoreboard. On that one Maruti is 93% of the whole thing. India's most successful electric car, measured by units leaving the country, is a car most Indian buyers have never seen on their street.

There is a reason for that split. Suzuki designed the e Vitara as a global model and picked India as the place to build it for the world. Europe and Japan were always the main targets. India got the factory first and the showroom focus second.

## What this means for you

Not much directly, if you are shopping for an EV this month. But it matters for what you will be able to buy in two years.

A plant building 50,000-plus cars for Europe has to meet European crash, software and quality rules every single day. That discipline does not stay in the export bay. It shows up in the cars sold here, and it gives Maruti a reason to keep investing in EV lines in India rather than treating electric as a side project.

It also changes the supplier maths. Battery packs, motors and power electronics get cheaper per unit when a factory is making them at export scale. Indian buyers eventually ride on volume someone else paid for.

## The EVGyan take

A 93% share sounds dominant, and it is, but it also says something about everyone else. India's other carmakers have barely started exporting EVs. That is the real headline hiding inside this number.

The country has spent years talking about becoming an EV manufacturing hub. One company, one model and one plant is a start, not a hub. The number to watch is not whether Maruti crosses 1 lakh exports, it is whether a second Indian-built EV shows up on that list at all.
